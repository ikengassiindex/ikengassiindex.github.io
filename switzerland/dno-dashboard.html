DSO Segmented Dashboard — Switzerland

Federalised 600+-utility market · ~4.5 M HV/MV connection points · 26 Kantone · ~1,250 substations (post-§38 OSM filter)

Switzerland's distribution market is the most-fragmented in the OECD: more than 600 utilities operate across the 26 Kantone, with the top six covering roughly 44% of customers and the long tail of ~550 cantonal and municipal operators carrying the rest. The transmission backbone is operated by Swissgrid as single TSO (380 / 220 kV, ~6,700 km), formed in 2009 through pooling of the previously cantonal grids. Switzerland sits outside the EU but is the densest cross-border node in continental Europe via 41 interconnection lines with 5 neighbours; the bidding zone has been CH standalone since the EU-CH electricity agreement stalled. Per KB §53, R3 socio-economic calibration uses a 4-tier schedule with Zurich + Basel-Stadt + Geneva as capital-intensive outliers (1.02) and the small rural Cantons (Uri, Glarus, Appenzell, Obwalden, Nidwalden, Schaffhausen) at 1.08.

The top 6 DSOs above cover ~44% of Swiss customers. The top-50 utilities cover ~80%. The long tail of ~550 cantonal and municipal utilities — the famous Swiss federalised model — accounts for the remaining ~20%, with sizes ranging from ~50,000 customers (e.g. EKZ in Zurich Cantons hinterland — ~290 k; SIG in Geneva — ~250 k; AEW Aargau — ~190 k; Groupe E in Fribourg — ~180 k; ewb Energie Wasser Bern — ~100 k) down to fewer than 200 customers in the smallest Talgemeinden. Total fleet: ~4.5 M residential + ~0.4 M commercial/industrial connection points across the 26 Kantone. Federalism here is constitutional: each Canton sets its own concession framework under the federal StromVG umbrella.

Below these sit ~550 smaller cantonal and municipal utilities including ewb Bern (~100 k), EKT Thurgau (~85 k), St.Galler Stadtwerke (~70 k), Onyx Energie Mittelland (~55 k), Energie Wasser Luzern (~50 k), and a long tail of valley-municipality operators serving from 200 to 30,000 customers each. This pattern — the most fragmented in the OECD — reflects the Swiss federal constitution and the historic practice of municipal energy democracy; ElCom regulates all of them under the StromVG framework with proportionate reporting cadence by size class.

TSO context — Swissgrid

Switzerland is a single-TSO country (Swissgrid, since 2009 cantonal grid pooling and 2013 ownership unbundling under the StromVG). Swissgrid owns and operates the 380 / 220 kV transmission backbone and acts as the balancing market operator. It is part of the Continental European synchronous area via bilateral exchange agreements as an ENTSO-E observer member (Switzerland is not an EU Member State; the 2025 EU-CH electricity agreement remains stalled at federal-Council level).

European peer comparison — DSO SAIDI 2024

Switzerland's national SAIDI ranks among Europe's lowest. The federal weighted average (~18 min) is held down by the urban best-in-class operators (EWZ 11, IWB 14, SIG 13) and by aggressive undergrounding programmes in the lake-shore corridors. Alpine outliers (Repower 34 min, Engadin valley feeders) pull the upper quartile but the absolute number remains tight by CEER benchmarking standards.

Strategic context — what to watch

2024 summer floods (R6c watchlist): Between June and August 2024, three flood events affected the Lake Lucerne, Linth and Saane catchments. Swissgrid recorded ~11 substation-hours of weather-driven outage but no asset losses. The Rhône (Wallis to Lake Geneva), Aare (Bern + Aargau), Rhine (Basel) and Ticino (south alpine) corridors carry the highest flood-band scores; R6c_flood alpha [0.02, 0.06] across the cohort. MeteoSwiss GHK (Gefahrenhinweiskarten) atlas is the authoritative national hazard layer used by SSI for flood proximity calibration.

Alpine seismicity (R6_seismic): Wallis (Valais) carries PGA 0.16 g at the 475-year return — the most seismic point in DACH. Basel + Graubünden + Ticino sit at 0.12 g. Northern Cantons (Aargau, Schaffhausen, Thurgau, Zürich) sit at 0.04–0.08 g. The 1946 Sierre quake (M_L 5.8) remains the largest instrumentally recorded Swiss event. SED (Swiss Seismological Service) operates 200+ permanent stations and publishes the ESHM20 + national PGA atlas. For SSI scoring, R6_seismic alpha band [0.04, 0.22] applies — the highest in DACH.

Nuclear phase-out (R5 generation mix): Mühleberg closed 2019; Beznau I+II, Gösgen, and Leibstadt are scheduled to phase out by 2034 per the 2017 referendum. Hydropower currently anchors 58% of CH generation (Axpo + Alpiq + BKW); pumped storage adds 5.3 GW (Linth-Limmern, Nant de Drance, Grimsel, Vieux-Émosson). The replacement bet is a combination of accelerated PV rollout, deeper pumped-storage utilisation, and selective HVDC interconnection upgrades — all of which raise distribution-system R3 hosting-capacity requirements.

Aletsch + Rhône glacier retreat: The Aletsch glacier is the largest in the Alps and the bellwether for climate-trajectory anchoring of Wallis substations. CMIP6 RCP4.5 projects 50% mass loss by 2050. Loss of glacial baseflow reshapes Rhône hydropower seasonality from snow-melt-anchored to rainfall-anchored, increasing summer-trough variability and shifting R6_climate weight from winter (snow loading) toward summer (drought-stress on hydropower availability).

R7 cyber posture: Switzerland NCSC (National Cyber Security Centre) mandates the ICT-Minimum Standard 2018 for critical infrastructure operators including all licensed grid actors. The CYBER 2025 programme expands this to deeper grid-sector specifics including SCADA segmentation and post-quantum cryptography roadmaps. There is no DESI score for Switzerland (non-EU); R7 ceiling is set at 1.05 based on NCSC posture and ENISA observer-track collaboration — mid-band by DACH standards (AT 1.04, CH 1.05, DE 1.06).
