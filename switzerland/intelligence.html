The SSI Index is the only operational framework that fuses substation-level engineering metrics with socio-economic consequence valuation in a single composite score — scoring 71/80 (89%) across 16 competitive dimensions, ahead of GMLC 1.1 (48/80), EPRI (40/80), and academic MCDA frameworks (41/80). Its engine processes 320,000+ source data points per run, executes ~12.5 million Monte Carlo simulations through a 20×20 Gaussian copula correlation matrix, and produces full uncertainty quantification across ~1,250 substations spanning 148 districts and 2,148 municipalities across the 26 Kantone.

Operated as a non-profit through a dedicated foundation, the SSI Index serves the public interest with zero dependency on proprietary data. All 95 variables are drawn from 35 verified public sources, making the methodology fully auditable and reproducible. The Index captures Switzerland's unique structural position — Stromdrehkreuz Europas: 41 cross-border lines with 5 neighbours (DE × 11, IT × 12, FR × 9, AT × 6, LI × 3), the most-interconnected per-population grid in the OECD. The hydropower-dominant generation mix (58% of national output, plus 5.3 GW pumped storage at Linth-Limmern + Nant de Drance + Grimsel + Vieux-Émosson) ties grid reliability to glacial baseflow. Switzerland's non-EU regulatory posture sustains continental synchronous operation via bilateral exchange agreements. This inaugural Swiss edition prioritises the critical Wallis Alpine Seismic Corridor, where Europe's highest 475-yr PGA (0.16 g at Sierre) intersects with the Rhône hydropower cascade.

Switzerland's position in Central Europe: Switzerland sits at the intersection of five European grid zones — German TenneT/TransnetBW/Amprion to the north, Italian Terna to the south, French RTE to the west, Austrian APG to the east, and Liechtenstein LKW to the east. Swissgrid (single TSO since 2009) manages the 380/220 kV transmission network spanning ~6,700 km across all 26 Kantone. The 41 cross-border lines make Switzerland the most-interconnected per-population grid in the OECD — and the most strategically important transit hub for North-South European power flows (DE hydropower exports → IT load centres). The 2023 ENTSO-E Winter Outlook flagged the CH bidding zone — operating standalone since the EU-CH electricity agreement stalled — as a structural constraint on continental price coupling. The SSI Index provides the substation-level intelligence that Swissgrid's Strategic Grid 2030/2040 and the post-nuclear-phase-out reinvestment programme require — identifying not regional aggregate problems, but specific assets that demand urgent action.

E.1   Switzerland in European Benchmarking

Renewable + Hydropower
Switzerland: ~62% renewable electricity (2023) — hydropower 58% (Axpo + Alpiq + BKW operate the cascade), solar 3%, wind <1%. The 5.3 GW pumped-storage fleet (Linth-Limmern + Nant de Drance + Grimsel + Vieux-Émosson) makes CH Europe's largest dispatchable storage system per capita. The Aletsch + Rhône glacier retreat (CMIP6 RCP4.5 projects 50% mass loss by 2050) introduces a forward-looking baseflow risk the SSI captures via R2 climate trajectory.
Cyber-Resilience (NCSC + CYBER 2025)
Switzerland is non-EU and outside DESI. R7 calibration uses NCSC + ICT-Minimum Standard 2018 + the 2025 CYBER expansion to grid sector. Smart-meter penetration ~70% nationally (BFE target 80% by 2027). The SSI's R7 modifier captures the rural/urban gradient — identifying alpine DSOs with weakest digital infrastructure, prioritising NCSC-coordinated modernisation targets.

E.2   Cross-Border Interconnection

Switzerland is the geographic and electrical linchpin of Central Europe. The 41 cross-border lines (DE × 11, IT × 12, FR × 9, AT × 6, LI × 3) make it the most-interconnected per-population grid in the OECD. North-South transit (German hydropower + nuclear → Italian load) routes through the Gotthard / San Bernardino / Simplon corridors; East-West transit (French nuclear → Austrian/German DACH) routes via the Rhône valley. The CH bidding zone has operated standalone since the EU-CH electricity agreement stalled — meaning CH manages cross-border capacity via the Swissgrid Auction Office bilaterally with each neighbour, rather than through EU price coupling. The SSI Index's R4 (Graph Criticality) modifier quantifies this precisely — identifying which Swiss substations are highest-impact on the European grid topology.
