Section D: Deep-Dive — Śląskie-Mazowieckie Industrial Corridor

The Śląskie-Mazowieckie corridor encompasses Poland's largest industrial agglomeration. Śląskie (Upper Silesia) is the coal heartland, with 15.3% of national substations and the highest median risk (0.487) due to mining subsidence. Mazowieckie (Warsaw metropolitan area) is the economic center, with the largest population-at-risk and critical infrastructure density. The corridor comprises ~380 substations — 22.5% of the national fleet — and represents systemic interdependency risk due to shared transmission bottlenecks.

Section E: Coal Transition Spotlight

Poland's Coal Paradox: Despite EU climate commitments (carbon neutrality by 2050), Poland remains Europe's largest hard coal producer and maintains 30% coal-fired generation capacity. The κ_coal_transition modifier captures stranding risk: substations feeding aging coal plants that face early retirement. Śląskie and Dolnośląskie are most exposed. However, the transition also presents opportunity: Poland is rapidly deploying wind (>10 GW capacity) and solar (>3 GW), creating grid modernization requirements.
