Colombia's electricity distribution market is the most fragmented in the OECD after the United States, with ~25+ Operadores de Red across four ownership categories: state-controlled (post-Electricaribe split into Air-e + Afinia/EPM 2020), municipal (EPM Medellín, EMCALI Cali), Enel-controlled (Codensa Bogotá+Cundinamarca), and cooperative/private regional. Post-Law 142+143/1994 unbundling, the sector is competitive in generation + retail with regulated distribution + transmission.

For simulation purposes the SIN is treated as semi-insular — Ecuador-coupled but Venezuela-decoupled — with a lower interconnect-buffer ratio than continental-European grids but non-zero. The ZNI (Zonas No Interconectadas — Amazon, Pacific, La Guajira frontier; ≈0.5% of population, diesel + mini-grid supply) sits outside the SIN scope and is excluded from the SSI substation count.

Cohort-Context Note — Fragmentation

Colombia's distribution sector is the most fragmented OECD distribution market after the United States, with ~25+ Operadores de Red across mixed state-controlled, municipal (EPM Medellín, EMCALI Cali), Enel-Colombia private (Codensa), and cooperative ownership. This contrasts with single-DSO-dominant cohort entries and complicates per-DSO data ingestion: CREG annual market reports and individual OR quality filings are the primary source channels, with XM Boletín Estadístico Mensual providing market-wide aggregates.
