🇨🇴Methodology — Colombia

How the SSI v4.0.2 score is calculated for Colombia — from raw data to final classification. Open methodology, open data, fully reproducible across 33 departamentos.

Breaking the Silos

Grid resilience in Colombia is inseparable from its hydro-dependent generation matrix, its active Andean seismic + volcanic regime, its internal armed-conflict legacy, and its socio-economic gradient running from Bogotá D.C. to the Amazon frontier. A substation serving a Chocó or Amazonas department with slow restoration, weak fiscal capacity, and proximity to a historical pylon-attack corridor is structurally more vulnerable than one with identical electrical characteristics in the Bogotá metro. The SSI captures this by fusing grid metrics with DANE socio-economic, IDEAM hydro-meteorological, SGC seismic + volcanic, ColCERT cyber, and Unidad de Víctimas + Migración Colombia displacement data — all from public sources.

Processing Pipeline
1

Ingest

95 variables from 30+ verified public Colombian sources — DANE (Departamento Administrativo Nacional de Estadística) census + departamento-level GDP per capita + multidimensional poverty (MPI), UPME (Unidad de Planeación Minero-Energética) transmission + generation expansion plans, CREG (Comisión de Regulación de Energía y Gas) tariff + reliability + market structure, XM market dispatch + reservoir-level monitoring + CSIRT-XM cyber posture, IDEAM (Instituto de Hidrología, Meteorología y Estudios Ambientales) ENSO advisories + flood-hazard maps + climate baselines, SGC (Servicio Geológico Colombiano) 475-yr PGA hazard maps + volcanic-observatory alert bulletins (OVSM Manizales for Nevado del Ruiz, OVSP Pasto for Galeras, OVSPop Popayán for Puracé), ColCERT national CERT + CONPES 3854/2016 + 3995/2020 cyber framework, Unidad de Víctimas (IDP cumulative register), Migración Colombia (Venezuelan refugee register), Ministerio de Defensa (pylon-attack incident reporting), OSM Overpass (substation + line geometry via `out geom`). Zero proprietary SCADA dependencies. Maximum ingestion frequency: daily.

2

Normalise

Four normalisation methods: Method A (robust fleet percentile P5/P95), Method B (standard fleet percentile), Method C (bounded rescaling), and Method D (categorical mapping). Inverted metrics for density measures where higher = better resilience.

3

Weight

6-level hierarchical weighting across 6 components and 20 metrics. Continuity dominates at 0.30, followed by Infrastructure (0.25) and Saturation (0.20). Weight budget validated by Sobol global sensitivity analysis.

4

Compose R_base

Weighted mean of normalised C/V/I/E component scores plus T_base produces the base resilience score, prior to modifiers.

5

Modify

Multiplicative modifiers adjust R_base for context: R3 (consequence + energy poverty — Colombia uses a 5-tier calibration reflecting the steep Bogotá-vs-Amazon GDP gradient), R4 (graph-theoretic criticality on the SIN backbone with ~17,000 km of 220+ kV circuits operated by ISA INTERCOLOMBIA), R6_seismic (Andean Nazca subduction zone + Bucaramanga seismic nest + 1999 Armenia Mw 6.2 cohort-anchor), R6_volcanic (SGC alert-level proximity multiplier; Galeras ORANGE +0.40 within 15 km; Nevado del Ruiz YELLOW +0.25 within 25 km — cohort-second highest after Iceland), R6_drought (reservoir-electricity nexus — ENSO-warm-phase reservoir-drawdown factor coupled to hydro_dependence per departamento), R6_armed_conflict (NEW cohort-first — department + 50-km historical-attack-corridor proximity), R6_flood (La Niña anchors + IDEAM flood-hazard map), R7 (cyber — ColCERT + sectoral CSIRT-XM since 2019; CONPES 3854/2016 + 3995/2020 framework; ceiling 1.030).

6

Monte Carlo + Sobol

10,000 iterations per substation using a 20×20 Gaussian copula correlation matrix. Captures measurement, spatial, and staleness uncertainty. Sobol first-order + total-order indices identify dominant drivers. Produces median, P5, P95, skewness, and P_critical per substation.

7

Classify

Four bands: Low (0.00–0.25), Medium (0.25–0.50), High (0.50–0.75), Critical (0.75–1.00). Alert flags trigger when any single component exceeds its P95 fleet threshold.

Anchors: 1992-1993 Apagón (90-day nationwide rationing; triggered Law 142+143/1994 unbundling); 2015-2016 strong El Niño; 2023-2024 strong El Niño with emergency CREG-101-009-2024 demand curtailment + reservoir levels <35% by March 2024. +0.10 V_climate for hydro_dependence > 40% (Cundinamarca, Huila, Santander); +0.18 for > 60% (Antioquia EPM portfolio: Guatapé / Ituango / Porce / San Carlos).
R6_armed_conflict [0.00, 0.15] COHORT-FIRST. Department + 50-km historical-attack-corridor proximity. Pre-2016 FARC-EP era: several thousand pylon attacks 1985-2002 (200-300/year peak mid-1990s). Post-2016 Peace Accord: ≈40-80 pylon attacks/year + 5-10 substation attacks/year (per Ministerio de Defensa). High-risk corridors: Catatumbo (Norte de Santander, ELN), Cauca/Nariño Pacific (dissident FARC), Bajo Cauca/Sur de Córdoba (Clan del Golfo), Arauca + Casanare (ELN historical). ~12% of fleet in elevated zone.
R6_flood [0.00, 0.10] La Niña ENSO-cool-phase anchors (2010-2011 floods, 2017 Mocoa landslide 333 fatalities) + IDEAM flood-hazard map per zone. +0.05 V_climate baseline for Pacific coast (Chocó), Andean foothills (Boyacá, Caldas), Caribbean (Atlántico, Bolívar); up to +0.10 in validated high-risk floodplain zones.

Uncertainty Quantification

Markov 8-state · Monte Carlo · Sobol

Every substation score includes a 90% confidence interval derived from 10,000 Monte Carlo iterations. The simulation uses a 20×20 Gaussian copula to preserve empirical correlations between metrics. Three uncertainty sources are modelled: measurement error, spatial disaggregation noise across 33 departamentos, and data staleness. An 8-state Markov degradation model (extending CIGRE TB 761) tracks asset condition from new to critical with ENSO-modulated transition rates. Sobol first-order + total-order indices identify dominant drivers for each region.

Competitive Positioning

v4.0.2 · Colombia

SSI v4.0.2 introduces with Colombia the cohort-first R6_armed_conflict modifier (durable infrastructure-targeting form, distinct from acute national-conflict readings elsewhere in the cohort). Colombia is also the cohort-first country with a predominantly-hydro electricity matrix subject to recurring El Niño drought stress, so the R6_drought modifier is recalibrated to a reservoir-electricity-nexus form. Active-volcanism exposure (R6_volcanic) reuses the engine introduced for Iceland with Colombia-specific α-calibration anchored on the 1985 Armero lahar. Acknowledged gaps remain: comprehensive sectoral incident reporting on R6_armed_conflict 2024-2025 attack counts (depends on Ministerio de Defensa release cadence) and the Cyber Security Law enactment status (still draft in Congreso as of mid-2024).
