A

Environmental — Carbon Intensity & Generation Matrix

Hydro-dominant matrix with ENSO-amplified thermal swings; coal phase-out 2030-2035; 12 GW renewable target by 2030 per UPME

Colombia's grid carbon intensity is structurally low in normal years (≈150 gCO₂/kWh) thanks to a ~70% hydro share, but jumps to 280-320 gCO₂/kWh during strong El Niño events as thermal-coal + thermal-gas ramp to cover reservoir-deficit hours. The 2023-2024 strong El Niño triggered emergency CREG-101-009-2024 demand-curtailment regulation and pushed reservoir levels below 35% by March 2024. Coal phase-out for domestic generation targets 2030-2035; Cerrejón and Drummond mine-mouth thermal (~120 MW combined) are the last in line. Renewable target: 12 GW by 2030 (La Guajira wind cluster + Cesar/Tolima/Atlántico solar).

B

Social — Displacement, Poverty, V_socio Gradient

Cohort-first mass-internal-displacement legacy + Venezuelan refugee context; per-departamento V_socio 0.20 (Chocó/Amazon) → 0.65 (Bogotá D.C.)

Colombia hosts the world's third-largest population of internally displaced persons — ≈9.4M IDPs cumulatively per the Unidad de Víctimas register — plus ≈3M Venezuelan refugees per Migración Colombia. Multidimensional poverty (MPI per DANE) sits at ≈16% nationally, but masks a sharp gradient: GDP/cap ranges from ~$2-3k in Chocó to ~$11-12k in Bogotá D.C. Rural electrification is high (≈99.5% cohort-leading for a developing economy), but quality-of-supply diverges by an order of magnitude — Chocó / Amazon / Llanos Orientales SAIDI 50-150 hr/yr vs Bogotá 5-8 hr/yr. V_socio per substation: Bogotá D.C. baseline 0.65 (high resilience); Antioquia 0.55; Caribbean coast 0.45; Chocó 0.20 (cohort-lowest social-resilience capacity); Amazon frontier 0.20-0.25.

C

Governance — Regulator + Planner + System Operator

CREG independent regulator · UPME planning · XM ISO-style market+dispatch · Ley 142+143/1994 unbundling · CONPES cyber framework

Colombia operates a fully unbundled electricity sector since the 1994 reform (Ley 142 — Public Services Law + Ley 143 — Electricity Law). CREG (Comisión de Regulación de Energía y Gas) is the statutory independent tariff + reliability + market-rules regulator under the Ministerio de Minas y Energía. UPME (Unidad de Planeación Minero-Energética) publishes long-term demand projections + the PERT transmission expansion plan + the PERG generation expansion plan. XM (Compañía de Expertos en Mercados, ISA subsidiary since June 1995) is the national system operator + wholesale-market operator (CND dispatch + ASIC market clearing) — ISO-style functional separation. The CONPES cyber framework (CONPES 3854/2016 + 3995/2020) sets national cyber-defence policy; CCOCI sits under MinDef; ColCERT is the national CERT.

D

Climate Vulnerability — Andean Glaciers, Amazon, ENSO

GAIN 2024 baseline ≈80/180 · Andean glacier retreat · Amazon deforestation · ENSO-amplified droughts and floods

Colombia ranks ≈80/180 on the 2024 GAIN climate vulnerability index — cohort-mid range. Germanwatch Climate Risk Index 2024 places Colombia ≈30 globally, with Andean glacier retreat (Sierra Nevada de Santa Marta, Nevados National Park) and Amazon deforestation as primary drivers. ENSO modulates grid stress in both directions: El Niño suppresses Pacific moisture transport to the Andes, drops reservoir inflows 30-50%, and shifts the hydro share from ~70% to ~50% (anchor: 1992-1993 Apagón, 2015-2016, 2023-2024). La Niña brings excess rainfall, flooding, and landslide risk (anchors: 2010-2011 nationwide floods; 2017 Mocoa landslide with 333 fatalities). NDC ambition 2030: 51% emissions reduction vs business-as-usual (per 2020 NDC update).

E

Cyber Posture — R7 Ceiling 1.030

ColCERT national CERT (2011) · CSIRT-XM sectoral electricity (2019) · CONPES 3854/2016 + 3995/2020 framework

Colombia's national cyber framework rests on CONPES 3854/2016 (Cyber Security Strategy) and CONPES 3995/2020 (Cyber Defence Policy Update). ColCERT has been operational since 2011 as the civilian-sector incident response body under joint MinTIC + Ministerio de Defensa governance. CSIRT-XM has operated the sectoral electricity CSIRT since 2019, financed by sector levy and mandated by CONPES 3854. The Cyber Defence Command (CCOCI) sits under MinDef. R7 in the SSI for Colombia carries a ceiling of 1.030 with a baseline of 1.020 — reflecting pre-crisis maturity (no major sectoral electrical-grid incident as of mid-2024). The Cyber Security Law remains in draft as of mid-2024 (pending Congreso 2025-2026 sessions).

F

Just Transition — Cesar & La Guajira Coal-Region Planning

Just Energy Transition Partnership tied to international finance · Drummond + Cerrejón phase-out

Colombia's just transition lens centres on Cesar (Valledupar; Drummond mining + emerging solar fleet) and La Guajira (Riohacha; Cerrejón coal mine + Jepirachi/Wayúu/Camelias/Carrizal/Apotolorrú wind cluster ~1.2 GW by 2024). A formal Just Energy Transition Partnership is being negotiated and tied to international climate finance, mirroring the JETP frameworks structured for South Africa, Indonesia, and Vietnam. The Drummond and Cerrejón coal operations remain export-focused but the domestic mine-mouth thermal share is targeted for phase-out 2030-2035 per the energy-transition policy. Wind cluster expansion in La Guajira and solar buildout in Cesar/Tolima/Atlántico are the cohort-most-visible Colombian transition stories.
