Overall Assessment: Sweden has excellent open data infrastructure comparable to Finland. SMHI (Swedish Meteorological and Hydrological Institute) operates ~700 weather stations, superior to FMI's ~200 (excellent Arctic/boreal coverage). SCB (Statistics Sweden) provides granular register-based statistics and demographic data annually. Svenska kraftnät publishes real-time generation and system adequacy data. Estimated coverage ~93% (slightly better than Finland due to SMHI's denser weather network). Main gaps: DSO fragmentation (~170 operators), substation vintage (pre-1980 records sparse), Arctic-specific equipment condition models, Kiruna mine subsidence infrastructure impacts.

Better in urban areas (Stockholm, Gothenburg, Malmö ~92%), good in northern regions (~80%). HIGH Cross-validate with Svenska kraftnät transmission map (published). Supplement with DSO data requests. GQ-3 H.4, H.5 (ISO 9223 corrosion) Corrosion class varies with coastal/industrial proximity: Coastal (Gothenburg, Malmö, Baltic Sea) C3–C4. Industrial zones C3. Inland C1–C2. Arctic climate does NOT increase corrosion. Permafrost in Norrbotten irrelevant for corrosion. MEDIUM Use air quality data (SMHI/Swedish EPA) for industrial corrosion. GQ-5 D4 (Flood risk) Spring flood potential: SMHI maps provide excellent baseline. Arctic thaw timing affects spring flood magnitude (Dalälven, Ångermanälven river systems). Climate change may extend flood season duration. Major river flood corridors well-documented. MEDIUM Model two scenarios: (1) Historical flood frequency (SMHI baseline). (2) Climate-enhanced (CMIP6 RCP4.5, 1.2–1.4× flood frequency by 2050, shifted season). Use SMHI real-time river monitoring. Flag major river corridors (Dalälven, Ångermanälven, Torneälven) as moderate uncertainty vs coastal areas.
GQ-6 C.6 (Migration) International + domestic migration: Sweden experiences migration from EU/Nordic countries (labor) and some refugee/asylum migration. SCB tracks accurately. 5. Sweden-Specific Gaps

Gap IDIssueImpactSeverityMitigation
SA-1 DSO fragmentation (~170 operators): Highly fragmented distribution system across 21 Län and 290 Kommune. Different IT systems, reporting standards, data formats. Consolidation minimal vs other Nordic countries. Metadata inconsistency, asset ID conflicts, regional comparison difficult. Data aggregation complexity high. Larger fragmentation challenge than Finland. HIGH Build DSO-specific ingestion pipelines. Ei regulatory filings provide unified view (annual). Request standardized data via Ei coordination. Document all reconciliation decisions. Target largest 50 DSOs first (cover ~85% of grid).
SA-2 Arctic equipment aging models (Norrbotten/Västerbotten): Standard CIGRÉ models assume temperate climate. Arctic conditions (cold, low humidity, permafrost risk in Norrbotten) create different aging profiles. Equipment condition curves (B3) may overestimate degradation for Arctic-adapted equipment. Transformer insulation ages slower in cold. Norrbotten ~14% of Swedish land area. MEDIUM Apply Arctic thermal adjustment: κ_thermal = 0.84–0.94 (vs standard 1.0). SMHI temperature norms validate. Cold climates preserve paper insulation. Widen literature search to Nordic utilities (Vattenfall SE/NO, Statnett) for Arctic calibration data.
SA-3 North-south transmission bottleneck (SE1→SE4 bidding zones): Sweden's power system divided into 4 bidding zones (SE1 north, SE2 middle, SE3 south, SE4 southeast). Chronic transmission constraints between zones. Nord-south price differentials reflect bottleneck. Grid capacity constraints affect VoLL (V1), system adequacy (S1), and congestion pricing. Historical load flow patterns may not reflect future bottlenecks as demand shifts (electrification, data centers). Bidding zone structure impacts regional resilience assessment. MEDIUM Use Svenska kraftnät bidding zone maps and historical price differentials. Model three scenarios: (1) Current topology (SE1–SE4 constraints). (2) New transmission capacity (planned lines). (3) Demand shift (electrification). Flag SE1→SE2 and SE3→SE4 corridors as critical for resilience. Monitor Vindkraft (wind) capacity growth in SE1 (north).
SA-4 Kiruna mine subsidence (LKAB iron ore): World's largest underground iron ore mine (LKAB, Norrbotten). Subsidence causing town relocation (Kiruna ~18,000 people). Affects ~2 km radius of substations/infrastructure. Unique localized hazard. Foundation destabilization risk for substations/poles near Kiruna. Subsidence depth ~1.2 m expected (2020s–2040s). Grid infrastructure may require relocation or hardening. Geological hazard H layer impact localized but severe. MEDIUM Identify substations within 2 km radius of Kiruna mine (est. ~5–10 substations). Request LKAB subsidence projections and timeline. Flag affected assets with higher structural risk multiplier (1.2–1.5× foundation failure probability). Monitor SGU/LKAB updates for subsidence progression. Plan infrastructure relocation contingency.
SA-5 Nuclear decommissioning (Forsmark, Ringhals, Oskarshamn): Sweden has 6 operating nuclear reactors at 3 sites. Decommissioning planned 2020s–2040s with extended timelines. Grid reconfiguration uncertainty. Nuclear plants supply ~40% of Swedish generation. Decommissioning will require substantial new capacity (renewable + grid storage). Grid topology may change (new interconnects, backup substations, transmission upgrades). MEDIUM Model three scenarios: (1) Extended operation through 2045. (2) Decommissioning 2030s–2040s (policy baseline). (3) Accelerated to 2030s (less likely). Svenska kraftnät publishes long-term infrastructure plans. Monitor Strålsäkerhetsmyndigheten (SSM, radiation authority) timeline and grid adaptation plans.
SA-6 Gotland island grid (separate, submarine cable): Gotland Islands (autonomous region with separate power system). Connected to mainland via two 70 km submarine cables (HVDC). Limited DSO coverage data. Island infrastructure fragile. ~60,000 population, small grid. Data sparse. Island infrastructure dependent on submarine cable reliability (rare failure but catastrophic). Island-specific vulnerability profile different from mainland. MEDIUM Treat Gotland separately. Request Gotland's DSO data directly. Island grid simpler (lower asset count ~200–300 substations). Apply island-specific fragility (submarine cable vulnerability, limited backup capacity). Cable failure = island blackout. Request cable reliability from Svenska kraftnät.
SA-7 Permafrost monitoring in Norrbotten/Lapland: Permafrost exists in northern Norrbotten (small area ~2% of land). Affects foundation stability for poles/towers. Thaw risk from climate change. Foundation degradation in permafrost zones. Pole instability risk elevated in high-latitude locations. Geological hazard H layer impact limited but localized. Permafrost line migrating southward (climate change). LOW SGU monitors permafrost (MAAT tracking, Abisko research site). Flag substations/lines in permafrost zones (latitude >66°N with peaty soils, north Norrbotten). Apply 1.1–1.2× foundation risk multiplier for affected assets. Risk limited geographically (~2% of land). Monitor migration of permafrost boundary (~50 m/decade southward).
