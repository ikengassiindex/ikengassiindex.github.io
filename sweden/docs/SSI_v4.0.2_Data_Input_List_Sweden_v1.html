4. Data Source Assessment by Tier

Tier 1: Primary Sources (10 sources, ~65% of variables)

Open access, high authority, Sweden-specific. SWEDEN-NATIVE sources (Svenska kraftnät, Energimarknadsinspektionen, SCB, SMHI, SGU, Naturvårdsverket, Elsäkerhetsverket, SSM, OSM, Copernicus).

Tier 2: Secondary Sources (10 sources, ~25% of variables)

Modeled, interpolated, or regional surveys. Lantmäteriet, Energimyndigheten, Länsstyrelserna, Energiföretagen Sverige, Riksbanken, DSO annual reports, Svensk Vindenergi, SLU, ENTSO-E, HELCOM.

Tier 3: Proxy / Fallback (2–3 sources, ~10% of variables)

Substation vintage (OSM start_date + cohort models), critical infrastructure (spatial analysis + population density), equipment condition (age curves + ambient temp + ice loading + nuclear proximity).

5. Key Advantages & Challenges

Advantages

  • Svenska kraftnät transparency: Public data portal with hourly dispatch, 4 bidding zones (SE1–SE4), cross-border flows with 14+ interconnections. Real-time Nordic market data integrated with ENTSO-E. Superior coverage vs. southern European TSOs.
  • Register-based Census (SCB): Sweden's continuous register system provides granular demographic updates at kommun level (290 units) without census gaps. Real-time employment, income, and migration tracking via tax registers.
  • SMHI world-class meteorology: ~700 weather stations with extensive historical records and high-quality forecasting. Superior ice phenology and snowmelt prediction vs. Central Europe. Open API access with no licensing barriers.
  • Comprehensive interconnection network: Sweden's 14+ cross-border links to Norway, Finland, Denmark, Germany, and Poland enable detailed flow analysis. ENTSO-E transparency platform provides EU-wide market context.
  • Wind energy leadership: Installed capacity ~18 GW (among EU top 5 producers); Svensk Vindenergi provides detailed regional breakdown by Län and technology type. Strong pipeline visibility for capacity planning.
  • SGU postglacial rebound expertise: Gulf of Bothnia experiencing ~9 mm/yr uplift — detailed mapping crucial for coastal and riverine infrastructure. Low seismic hazard simplifies foundation risk assessment.

Challenges

  • DSO fragmentation: ~170 active DSOs with highly variable reporting standards and asset transparency. Energimarknadsinspektionen aggregates some metrics, but granular asset-level data inconsistent across operators (Vattenfall Eldistribution vs. regional players).
  • North-south transmission bottleneck: Critical constraint (SE1 → SE4 congestion) during winter peak demand and summer export periods. Limited real-time line rating data; SCADA visibility restricted to Svenska kraftnät internal systems.
  • Kiruna mine subsidence: Northern mining operations causing localized ground movement affecting infrastructure in Norrbotten. Requires collaboration with mining companies and county boards for spatial monitoring.
  • Nuclear decommissioning timelines: Aging fleet with uncertain decommissioning schedules (Barsebäck, Ringhals). SSM data on operational timelines less predictive due to political/economic uncertainty. Proximity hazard data evolving.
  • Substation vintage data gaps: OSM coverage strong (~85% HV) but OSM start_date completeness variable in rural areas. Individual DSO asset databases not universally public; age cohort models required.
  • Hydropower volatility: Sweden's ~40% hydropower penetration creates generation variability tied to precipitation and snowmelt timing. Historical patterns less predictive as climate shifts seasonal dynamics.
  • Regional disparities: Dense urban concentration (Stockholm, Gothenburg, Malmö) vs. sparse infrastructure in Norrland and Västsverige. Service reliability and asset density vary dramatically by Län.

6. Estimated Timeline & Ingestion Phases

All 95 variables status: PLANNED (pre-ingestion phase). Once data ingestion commences, expect phased rollout over 4–6 months:
  • Phase 1 (Week 1–4): Core sources (D01–D10): Svenska kraftnät, Energimarknadsinspektionen, SCB, SMHI, SGU, Naturvårdsverket, Elsäkerhetsverket, SSM, OSM. ~60 variables mapped to 21 Län + 4 bidding zones.
  • Phase 2 (Week 5–8): Secondary tier (D11–D17): Lantmäteriet, Energimyndigheten, Länsstyrelserna, Energiföretagen Sverige, Riksbanken, DSO reports, Svensk Vindenergi, SLU. ~25 variables for 290 Kommuner.
  • Phase 3 (Week 9–16): Markov model (F/I), Monte Carlo engine, validation across ~5,000 HV substations. Nordic market integration (ENTSO-E, HELCOM, cross-border flow analysis). North-south bottleneck modeling.
  • Phase 4 (Week 17–24): Dashboard deployment, ESG enrichment, intelligence page monthly updates. Cross-border coordination testing (Norway, Finland, Denmark). Mining subsidence and nuclear decommissioning scenario planning.
