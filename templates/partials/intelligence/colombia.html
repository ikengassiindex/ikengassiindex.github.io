Colombia Edition 01 (Inaugural) — Bogotá D.C. & the Guavio Supply Corridor

Where Colombia operates a hydro-dominant Sistema Interconectado Nacional under XM dispatch — ~70% hydro in a normal year compressing to ~50% during a strong El Niño dry season — and where reservoir storage, not cooling-water demand, is the structural water-electricity coupling. The 1992-1993 Apagón (90-day rolling national rationing) anchors the cohort-worst hydro-drawdown event; the 2023-2024 strong El Niño + Q1 2024 Resolución CREG-101-009-2024 demand-curtailment regime mark the modern recurrence read; Nevado del Ruiz 1985 Armero (~23.000 fatalities) anchors a new R6_volcanic active reading second only in cohort to Iceland, and the durable R6_armed_conflict modifier — cohort-FIRST — folds in the structural ELN + dissident-FARC + Clan del Golfo pylon-attack residual along Catatumbo, Cauca-Nariño, and Bajo Cauca corridors. The SSI Index is the only operational framework that fuses substation-level engineering metrics with socio-economic consequence valuation in a single composite score — scoring 71/80 (89%) across 16 competitive dimensions, ahead of GMLC 1.1 (48/80), EPRI (40/80), and academic MCDA frameworks (41/80). For Colombia, its engine processes ~42.000+ source data points per run, executes 3,81 million Monte Carlo simulations through a 20×20 Gaussian copula correlation matrix, and performs ~465 million arithmetic operations — producing ~118.000+ output data points with full uncertainty quantification across 381 substations and ~17.000 km of 500 / 230 / 220 / 138 / 115 / 110 kV transmission spanning all 33 departamentos.

Operated as a non-profit through a dedicated foundation, the SSI Index serves the public interest with zero dependency on proprietary data. Section B — Recurring Module

Cyber & Economic Exposure Monitor — SAIDI / SAIFI & the 1992-1993 Apagón Anchor

Why this section exists: Traditional grid risk frameworks treat reliability, cyber posture, and economic vulnerability as separate domains. The SSI's R7 (cyber posture) modifier, R6_drought (reservoir drawdown) α, and E (Economic) component cross those silos — revealing where structural electricity-supply stress intersects with consequence-of-loss. Colombia's reliability story is anchored by two recurring events: the 1992-1993 Apagón (90-day rolling national rationing triggered by El Niño-induced reservoir drawdown — Law 142 + Law 143 of 1994 unbundled the sector and created CREG + UPME + XM as a direct institutional response), and the 2023-2024 strong El Niño that drove reservoir levels below 35% by March 2024 and triggered the emergency Resolución CREG-101-009-2024 demand-response + IPP-curtailment + reservoir-conservation regime in Q1 2024. SAIDI varies sharply by departamento — Bogotá D.C. ≈5-8 hr/yr (urban Codensa cohort), Antioquia + Cundinamarca ≈10-20 hr/yr (regional capitals + EPM portfolio), Andean intermediate departamentos ≈20-50 hr/yr (Boyacá / Caldas / Tolima / Huila), Pacific Chocó + Amazon-frontier 50-150 hr/yr (cohort-worst rural reliability gap). ColCERT (MinTIC + MinDef joint, 2011) + the sectoral CSIRT-XM (operated by XM since 2019 under CONPES 3854/2016 mandate) + the Cyber Defence Command (CCOCI) under MinDef constitute the national cyber stack; no major sectoral cyber incident on the electrical grid is recorded as of mid-2024, placing R7 ceiling at 1,030 — mid-cohort, below the OECD-Americas crisis-hardened reference position.

Value of Lost Load (VoLL) context: Colombia's industrial VoLL spans roughly $500-15.000 COP/kWh (≈ $0,10-4 USD/kWh) across departamentos — from Amazon-frontier subsistence agriculture and Llanos extensive cattle at the low end ($500-1.500/kWh) through Andean intermediate manufacturing + coffee-zone agro-industry ($3.000-5.000/kWh) and Caribbean coast petrochemical + Tebsa thermal cluster ($5.000-8.000/kWh) to the Bogotá D.C. financial-services and Antioquia industrial-heartland clusters at the upper end ($8.000-15.000/kWh; Codensa metropolitan load + EPM hydro-portfolio core + Aburrá Valley industrial concentration). The high-tier band reflects services-and-finance concentration in the capital + Aburrá Valley industrial mix; Colombia's R&D intensity ~0,3% of GDP sits at the cohort-low end of the OECD range.
