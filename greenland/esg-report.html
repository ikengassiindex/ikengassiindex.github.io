Climate Physical Risk Assessment

ESRS E1 · TCFD Physical Risk · EU Taxonomy Annex A
PARTIAL · Edition 001
Why this report exists

ESRS E1 (Climate Change) is material for 98% of CSRD-reporting companies. TCFD physical risk disclosure is mandatory in 36+ jurisdictions. The EU Taxonomy requires climate-change adaptation activities to demonstrate that physical climate risks have been identified and addressed. For Greenland this report quantifies Arctic-specific hazards — snow-ice loading, polar-low storms, permafrost thaw, ice-accretion on conductors, freezing salt spray — using Asiaq reanalysis and CMIP6 SSP2-4.5 downscaling, and Markov 5-state degradation.

SDG Alignment

SDG 13 — Climate Action (primary)

Target 13.1 calls for strengthening resilience and adaptive capacity to climate-related hazards. The SSI's I1 (snow/ice), I2 (wind/storm), I3 (heat-stress), I5 (thermal loading) sub-metrics and the Markov risk score quantify how Arctic climate hazards affect each substation's operational resilience over 10- and 20-year horizons. In Edition 001, I1/I2/I5 drive the Arctic R6b storm/surge overlay (α ∈ [0.05, 0.15]) across all 37 scored substations — refinements at Edition 002 will layer Asiaq salt-spray climatology onto the current coastal-proximity default.

Grid Equity & Social Vulnerability

ESRS S1/S2 · SFDR PAI Social Indicators · UN PRI
READY · Edition 001
Why this report exists

ESRS S2 requires disclosure of impacts on affected communities; SFDR mandates 5 social PAI indicators. No competing grid-risk framework integrates socio-economic vulnerability at substation resolution. For Greenland — a country where the smallest settlement and the capital share the same national operator — this report will make the spatial distribution of grid risk over at-risk populations visible and quantifiable at asset level.

SDG Alignment

SDG 7 — Affordable & Clean Energy (primary)

Target 7.1 requires universal access to affordable, reliable energy. Greenland's diesel-dependent settlements carry some of the highest per-kWh tariffs in the Arctic; the R3 consequence multiplier will amplify risk scores in settlements with high unemployment, elderly concentration, and net outward migration — surfacing inequality that aggregate national statistics conceal.

Energy Transition & DER Stress

ESRS E1 Transition Plan · TCFD Transition Risk · EU Green Deal
PARTIAL · Edition 001
Why this report exists

Greenland's electricity mix is overwhelmingly hydropower (~70%) with residual diesel generation in settlements not reachable by hydro interconnect. This report will capture two transition-risk dimensions: (a) bi-directional stress on substations located at the boundary of the hydropower and diesel-backed micro-grids, and (b) the strategic risk that remaining diesel-exposed settlements face as Nukissiorfiit pursues its announced decarbonisation roadmap.

SDG Alignment

SDG 7 — Affordable & Clean Energy (primary)

Target 7.2 requires substantially increasing renewable share. The T1_score measures transition stress at each substation — Greenland's fleet will show very low EV load (near zero) but highly heterogeneous DER penetration (high in hydro-connected settlements, zero in diesel-backed ones).

Pollution & Corrosion

ESRS E2 Pollution · ISO 9223 Corrosion Classification
PARTIAL · Edition 001
Why this report exists

Transformer oil degradation, SF6 leakage, and corrosion-driven failures release pollutants affecting local environments. Greenland's substations are almost all coastal — ISO 9223 C4 (high corrosion) is applied fleet-wide in Edition 001; Edition 002 will refine per-asset using Asiaq salt-spray climatology and distance-to-coast. Permafrost thaw at the substation foundation creates an additional leakage pathway specific to Arctic deployment.

SDG Alignment

SDG 11 — Sustainable Cities & Communities (primary)

Target 11.6 aims to reduce the adverse environmental impact of cities. The ISO 9223 corrosion class and E component will track environmental degradation and pollution sensitivity at asset level once Session 1 runs.

Cybersecurity Exposure

NIS2 Directive · ENISA Cybersecurity Index · ESRS G1 Governance
PARTIAL · Edition 001
Why this report exists

The NIS2 Directive mandates cybersecurity risk management for energy operators; as the kingdom's essential service provider, Nukissiorfiit is in scope via the EU directive's extraterritorial reach into Denmark. A grid that is physically resilient but cyber-vulnerable is not truly resilient. The SSI R7 modifier combines SCADA assessment, communication-architecture analysis, and national-level cyber maturity (ENISA / DESI) into a substation-level digital vulnerability score.

SDG Alignment

SDG 9 — Industry, Innovation, Infrastructure (primary)

Target 9.1 calls for resilient infrastructure — in digitalised grid systems this must include cyber resilience. R7 (pending Session 1) assesses digital vulnerability combining SCADA exposure, protocol security, and national cyber-maturity indicators.

Technical Validity

R7_cyber is computed from a weighted combination of (1) national cyber maturity via ENISA Cybersecurity Index and EU DESI connectivity indicators (Denmark proxy with adjustment for Greenland's Tele Greenland broadband reach), (2) graph-topology vulnerability (BC percentile = single-point-of-failure risk), and (3) SCADA protocol exposure assessment.
