DSO Segmented Dashboard — Austria

Federalised 6-DSO market · ~4.7 M HV/MV customers · 9 Bundesländer · 1,406 substations (1,144 HV + 262 MV)

Austria's distribution market is organised along Bundesland lines: each federal state has a dominant DSO, with three of them (Wiener Netze, Netz NÖ, Energie AG OÖ) covering roughly 60% of the country. The transmission backbone is operated by APG (Austrian Power Grid) as single TSO (380 / 220 / 110 kV), part of the Continental European synchronous area. Austria participates in the Nord Pool AT bidding zone (10Y1001A1001A831). Per KB §52, R3 socio-economic calibration uses a 4-tier schedule with Vienna as the capital-intensive outlier (1.02) and Burgenland + Carinthia as light-rural (1.06–1.08).

TSO context — APG (Austrian Power Grid)

Austria is a single-TSO country (APG, since 2012 ownership unbundling). APG owns and operates the 380 / 220 / 110 kV transmission backbone and acts as the balancing market operator. It is part of the Continental European synchronous area (no BRELL legacy, no recent re-synchronisation event).

European peer comparison — DSO SAIDI 2024

Austria's national SAIDI ranks among Europe's lowest — partly because urban Vienna (Wiener Netze ~22 min) pulls the weighted average down, partly because alpine-feeder concentration in Salzburg + Tyrol + Carinthia is offset by aggressive undergrounding programmes (52–92 % underground share by DSO). The CEER Benchmarking Report 2024 has Austria in the top quartile of European peers on continuity.

Strategic context — what to watch

Westring completion (2027): The 380 kV Salzburg–St. Peter–Tauern–Lienz–Kainachtal corridor is APG's largest current capital project. Completion in 2027 closes the long-standing east-west bottleneck and unlocks ~3 GW of additional north-south flow capacity. Pre-completion: Salzburg–Carinthia is the most-constrained interface during winter peak load and during major Alpine-wind events.

Alpine flooding (R6c watchlist): The 2024 Lower Austria + Vienna floods (storm Boris, mid-September) tested the Danube floodplain corridor; Netz NÖ recorded ~24 substation-hours of weather-driven outage but no asset losses. Wachau valley substations and Inn-confluence (IKB) assets carry the highest flood-band scores; R6c_flood alpha [0.02, 0.06] across the cohort.

Alpine seismicity (R6_seismic): Tyrol + Vorarlberg + Carinthia carry PGA 0.10 g (475-year return), the highest in Austria. Salzburg Tauern band 0.06–0.08 g. The 1972 Seebenstein earthquake remains the largest instrumentally recorded Austrian event (M_L 5.3). For SSI scoring, R6_seismic alpha band [0.04, 0.18] applies — middle-of-cohort within DACH (CH Wallis goes higher to 0.22).

Smart-meter rollout completion (2027): Six of the 9 Bundesländer hit > 95 % by end of 2024 (Wien, Tirol, Salzburg, Burgenland, Vorarlberg, Oberösterreich). Niederösterreich + Steiermark + Kärnten complete by 2027 per E-Control mandate. Implication for SSI: dynamic-demand-response readiness scoring moves from "deployment-phase" (R7 ceiling 1.04) to "operational" by 2027.

R7 cyber posture: Austria DESI 2024 = 0.65 (mid-high). E-Control mandates a 2-year cyber penetration-test cycle for transmission assets; NIS2 transposition completed Q4 2024 (timely). R7 ceiling 1.04 across the AT fleet — below DACH median (DE 1.06, CH 1.05). Reflects the small-fleet + high-coordination posture of a 9 M-population country with a single TSO and ~10 DSO market structure.
