Methodology

How the SSI v4.0.2 is calculated for Israel — from raw IEC, Noga, PUA, GSI, IMS, INCD, and CBS feeds through to the substation-level Rfinal. Open methodology, open data, fully reproducible.

Breaking the Silos

Power-grid resilience is inseparable from its socio-economic context. A mehoz serving a Galilee Arab municipality with long radial 22 kV feeders, slow restoration times and weaker fiscal capacity is fundamentally more vulnerable than a Tel Aviv urban-core substation with identical electrical characteristics. The SSI captures this reality by fusing traditional grid metrics — IEC asset condition, PUA SAIDI/SAIFI, Noga dispatch frequency — with socio-economic, environmental, and energy-transition data — all from public sources. Israel adds two cohort-first stress dimensions: a strike-slip transform fault (the Dead Sea Transform) as the primary seismic source, and an arid-climate drought modifier coupled to electricity demand through the desalination-water-electricity nexus.

Processing Pipeline
1

Ingest

95 variables from 30+ verified public data sources — IEC annual reports + transmission plan 2023-2030, Noga ILITO monthly + SMP wholesale market data (open since the November 2021 reform), PUA tariff resolutions + quality-of-service indicators (2023-24 annual report), CBS regional accounts at the mehoz level, Bank of Israel macro + ILS FX, IMS (Israel Meteorological Service) climate + drought indices, GSI (Geological Survey of Israel) seismology + 475-yr PGA hazard maps, INCD + CERT-IL critical-infrastructure scope, Mekorot national water utility + desalination plant electricity-demand data, MoEP air-quality monitoring, Copernicus ERA5 + CMIP6, IEA + OECD energy benchmarks, OSM Overpass (ISO3166-1='IL'). Israel is NOT an ENTSO-E member (effectively-insular Mediterranean grid; EuroAsia Interconnector under construction, target 2027-2028). Zero proprietary SCADA dependencies. Maximum ingestion frequency: daily.

2

Normalise

Four normalisation methods: Method A (robust fleet percentile P5/P95), Method B (standard fleet percentile), Method C (bounded rescaling), and Method D (categorical mapping). Inverted metrics for density measures where higher = better resilience. Hebrew-source feeds (IEC, PUA, CBS) are transliterated to Latin in dashboard rendering per `language_mode: 'latin_only'`; the original Hebrew labels are preserved in the source layer only.

3

Weight

6-level hierarchical weighting across 6 components and 20 metrics. Continuity dominates at 0.30, followed by Infrastructure (0.25) and Saturation (0.20). Weight budget validated by Sobol sensitivity analysis on the Israeli substation fleet.

4

Compose R_base

Weighted sum of 6 normalised component scores produces the base resilience score.

5

Modify

Seven multiplicative modifiers adjust R_base for context: R2 (adaptive climate IRI — at ~31-33°N Mediterranean-to-arid latitudes summer heat-wave loading + AC peak is structurally high; winter is mild), R3 (consequence + energy poverty — Israel uses a 4-tier calibration reflecting the Tel Aviv-to-Negev GDP gradient at 1.06 / 1.05 / 1.04 / 1.03), R4 (graph-theoretic network criticality on an effectively-insular grid with zero synchronous interconnects), R6_seismic (Dead Sea Transform strike-slip — cohort-first transform-fault primary source), R6_volcanic (formally inactive — Israel has no active volcanoes; preserved in stack with `active: false` flag for cohort consistency), R6_drought (NEW α anchor — Negev arid + desalination electricity-load multiplier), R7 (digital readiness, cohort-LEADING ceiling 1.040 reflecting INCD + Unit 8200 + CERT-IL posture).

6

Monte Carlo

10,000 iterations per mehoz using a 20×20 Gaussian copula correlation matrix. Captures measurement, spatial, and staleness uncertainty. Produces median, P5, P95, skewness, and P_critical for each Israeli substation.

7

Classify

Four bands: Low (0.00–0.25), Medium (0.25–0.50), High (0.50–0.75), Critical (0.75–1.00). Alert flags trigger when any single component exceeds its P95 fleet threshold.

R2 Adaptive IRI + Climate internal Shifts weight from climate-IRI to structural metrics. CMIP6 SSP2-4.5 projections drive forward-looking risk; at Israel's Mediterranean-to-arid latitudes, summer heat-wave + AC peak load is the dominant climatic stressor, and the precipitation gradient runs from ~600-800 mm in Galilee to ~25-200 mm in the Negev.
R3 Consequence + Poverty [0.70, 1.30] Amplifies risk for mehozot serving large or energy-poor populations with high economic dependency. Israel uses a 4-tier calibration: Capital-Tech 1.06 (Tel Aviv — Silicon Wadi HQ concentration, TASE, Check Point, Wix, Monday.com, Fiverr) · Industrial-Tech 1.05 (Central + Haifa — Sharon Plain corridor, Rosh HaAyin, Bazan refinery + Carmel chemical, Yokne'am chip cluster) · Capital-Services 1.04 (Jerusalem — Knesset, Bank of Israel, Hebrew University, Mobileye HQ, Hadassah) · Periphery 1.03 (Northern Galilee + Southern Negev — agriculture, Dead Sea Works, Dimona, Beersheba CyberSpark, Eilat). Distribution 1/2/1/2 across 6 mehozot.
R4 Graph Criticality [0.80, 1.35] Penalises topological bottlenecks: high betweenness centrality, bridge nodes, low degree. Built from OSM power graph filtered on ISO3166-1='IL'. Israel is effectively insular: `cross_border_lines: []` at first edition; the EuroAsia Interconnector (HVDC 2,000 MW IL-CY-GR, Nexans cable contract awarded, target 2027-2028) is documented as forward outlook and will be added to the topology graph at commissioning.
R6a Restoration Speed [0.90, 1.10] PUA-quality-report-based: rewards fast-restoring urban areas (Jerusalem ~24 min average restoration; Dan-Tel Aviv core ~59 min), penalises slow-restoring periphery (Negev sub-regions up to ~241 min in non-metropolitan zones per State Comptroller 2024). The 4.1× metro:periphery ratio is one of the steeper gradients in the cohort.
R6_seismic Network Topology + Seismic [1.00, 1.20] Network centrality + Dead Sea Transform (DST) strike-slip seismic hazard — cohort-first transform-fault primary source. The DST is the active African-Arabian plate boundary running north-south through the Arava → Dead Sea → Jordan River → Hula Valley, slip rate ~5 mm/yr. Modern anchor: 1927 Jericho earthquake Mw 6.2 (11 July 1927; ~350-500 deaths; damage Jerusalem + Nablus + Amman). Regional companion: 1995 Aqaba Mw 7.2 (Gulf of Eilat, southern DST). Pre-instrumental anchor: 1837 Galilee earthquake (Safed/Tiberias catastrophic); the 1837-to-present interval of ~190 years is at or past the modelled 80-150-yr recurrence on northern DST segments — "overdue major event" hypothesis is widely discussed in Israeli seismology. R6_seismic α band [0.10, 0.16]: Northern (Galilee + Hula on/near DST) 0.14-0.16 · Jerusalem 0.13-0.15 · Southern (Arava/Negev on southern DST) 0.11-0.13 · Tel Aviv coastal 0.10-0.11 · Haifa coastal 0.11-0.13 · Central 0.10-0.12. GSI publishes the 475-yr PGA hazard maps.
R6_volcanic Volcanic Activity [1.00, 1.00] INACTIVE — Israel has no active volcanoes (last activity Pliocene basalts of the Golan Heights and Mt Hermon plateau, ~4 Mya; geological-historical only, not active hazard). R6_volcanic is preserved in the modifier stack with `active: false` flag for cohort schema consistency, but does not contribute to R_final. This is the first precedent-setting use of the inactive-flag non-erasure pattern: the modifier module remains schema-loaded but contributes zero to the engine, maintaining parity with active-volcanism cohort peers without code-path activation.
R6_drought Arid-Climate Drought + Desalination Load (NEW) [1.00, 1.18] FIRST IN COHORT — Israel is the first country where arid-climate drought is a primary R6 anchor coupled to electricity demand via the desalination-water-electricity nexus. Mechanism: arid baseline (Negev precipitation 25-200 mm/yr vs Galilee 600-800 mm/yr) → desalination provides ~85% of municipal water → five major plants (Sorek + Hadera + Ashkelon + Palmachim + Soreq B) consume ~3.4% of national electricity at baseline → drought intensification (e.g. 2013-2018 multi-year drought) → desalination capacity utilised more aggressively → electricity-load multiplier rises → summer-peak stress increases. Distinct from any hydropower-deficit mechanism (Israel has no hydropower) and from any glacial-flood mechanism (Israel has no glaciers). α band [0.08, 0.18]: Southern (Negev arid; Beersheba; Dimona; Mitzpe Ramon) 0.16-0.18 · Central (coastal Sorek + Palmachim + Hadera plants) 0.12-0.14 · Haifa (coastal + Bazan industrial water) 0.11-0.13 · Tel Aviv (coastal, lower agricultural water) 0.10-0.12 · Jerusalem (highland, Kinneret-fed) 0.09-0.11 · Northern (Galilee, less drought-stressed) 0.08-0.10.
R7 Digital Readiness + Cyber Posture [0.99, 1.040] Cohort-LEADING ceiling. Reflects: INCD (Israel National Cyber Directorate, established Dec 2017 via Government Resolution 3270, under the Prime Minister's Office); CERT-IL (operational 2014, based at CyberSpark Beersheba alongside Ben-Gurion University); Unit 8200 (IDF military SIGINT, 1952 lineage — functionally equivalent to US NSA / UK GCHQ; talent-pipeline backbone of Israel's ~350+ cyber companies and ~10% of the global cyber industry). Stress anchors: Stuxnet 2010 (state-level cyber-physical attack on Iranian centrifuges, attribution widely associated with US-Israel cooperation); 2020 Iran water-utility cyber incident (attribution debated; reported attack on Mekorot SCADA, response widely attributed to Israeli authorities); Hezbollah rocket campaign Nov 2023 – Nov 2024 (ceasefire); Iran missile + drone barrages 13/14 April 2024 + 1 October 2024 (Arrow + David's Sling + Iron Dome interception). R7 1.040 reflects continuous-state high posture plus active-conflict context — the `is_high_threat_grid: true` country-config flag absorbs the kinetic-stress dimension without creating a new R6_terror modifier.

Uncertainty Quantification

Monte Carlo engine

Every Israeli substation score includes a 90% confidence interval derived from 10,000 Monte Carlo iterations. The simulation uses a 20×20 Gaussian copula to preserve empirical correlations between metrics (e.g., ρ(C1, E1) ≈ 0.70-0.85). Three uncertainty sources are modelled: measurement error, spatial disaggregation noise, and data staleness — the latter is especially relevant during active-conflict periods when PUA reliability bulletins lag the live grid state.

Forward Outlook — EuroAsia Interconnector & Insular-to-Connected Transition

2027-2028

Israel's grid is documented at first edition as effectively insular: `is_insular_grid: true`, `cross_border_lines: []`. The Egypt link at Eilat-Taba (~80 MW DC, intermittent post-2011) and the Jordan link (bilateral gas-for-electricity swaps, few hundred MW intermittent) are not synchronous and do not enter the topology graph. The EuroAsia Interconnector (Great Sea Interconnector) is a 1,000-2,000 MW HVDC subsea cable — 310 km Israel-Cyprus + 898 km Cyprus-Greece, 1,208 km total, depths up to 3,000 m, the world's longest + deepest HVDC subsea cable at commissioning. The Nexans cable contract has been awarded. Target operational entry 2027-2028 for the Israel leg. When commissioned, the project will end Israel's grid insularity mid-cohort-lifecycle and trigger an R4 + topology re-baselining; the methodology will switch from `cross_border_lines: []` to a populated array, and the R6_drought + R7 modifiers will not change.

OSM Extraction Boundary Notes

Transparency

Substations are extracted via Overpass on the ISO3166-1='IL' area filter. This is a tagging convention and not a political endorsement. Coverage: within the Green Line + East Jerusalem (Israeli civil law since the 1980 Basic Law) + Golan Heights (Israeli civil law since 1981, internationally disputed) — INCLUDED per the ISO tag. West Bank Areas A/B/C: partial — IEC-served facilities tagged 'IL' are included; PA-tagged facilities are excluded. Gaza: EXCLUDED — pre-October-2023 substations are not counted toward the Israeli fleet for transparency; IEC formally cut supply per government decision in March 2025. The SSI v4.0.2 documentation uses neutral language on disputed political matters.
