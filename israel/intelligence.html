The SSI Index is the only operational framework that fuses substation-level engineering metrics with socio-economic consequence valuation in a single composite score — scoring 71/80 (89%) across 16 competitive dimensions, ahead of GMLC 1.1 (48/80), EPRI (40/80), and academic MCDA frameworks (41/80). For Israel, its engine processes ~28,000+ source data points per run, executes 2.57 million Monte Carlo simulations through a 20×20 Gaussian copula correlation matrix, and performs ~310 million arithmetic operations — producing ~78,000+ output data points with full uncertainty quantification across 257 substations and ~1,100 grid lines spanning all 6 mehozot.

Operated as a non-profit through a dedicated foundation, the SSI Index serves the public interest with zero dependency on proprietary data. All 95 variables are drawn from 30+ verified public sources — IEC annual reports + transmission plan 2023-2030, Noga ILITO monthly + SMP wholesale market data (open since the November 2021 unbundling), PUA tariff resolutions + 2023-24 quality-of-service report, CBS regional accounts at the mehoz level, Bank of Israel macro + ILS FX, IMS climate + drought indices, GSI seismology + 475-yr PGA hazard maps along the Dead Sea Transform, INCD + CERT-IL critical-infrastructure scope, Mekorot national water-utility data + desalination-plant electricity-demand feeds (Sorek, Hadera, Ashkelon, Palmachim, Soreq B), MoEP air-quality monitoring — making the methodology fully auditable and reproducible. Israel is the inaugural single-country onboarding of Session 34 (post-CR), the first Middle-Eastern OECD member in the SSI v4 cohort, the first effectively-insular Mediterranean grid in cohort, and the first onboarding to introduce the NEW R6_drought α anchor and the precedent-setting R6_volcanic `active: false` flag pattern.

Cyber & Economic Exposure Monitor

Why this section exists: Traditional grid risk frameworks treat cybersecurity and economic vulnerability as separate domains. The SSI's R7 (cyber posture) modifier and E (Economic) component allow us to cross these silos — revealing substations where digital-resilience headwinds intersect with high economic consequence. For Israel, the framing inverts the usual cohort narrative: the cyber posture is cohort-LEADING (R7 ceiling 1.040 via INCD 2017 + Unit 8200 IDF lineage since 1952 + CERT-IL 2014 + the Beersheba CyberSpark ecosystem with ~350+ private cyber companies and ~10% of the global cyber industry), and the relevant question is which mehozot combine that structural cyber strength with high consequence-of-loss in a continuous-active-threat environment — Hezbollah rocket campaign Nov 2023 – Nov 2024 ceasefire, Iran missile + drone barrages 13/14 April 2024 + 1 October 2024 (Arrow + David's Sling + Iron Dome interception), and the broader post-Oct-7 grid-as-target context absorbed via the `is_high_threat_grid: true` country-config flag.

B.2   Economic Impact by Business Fabric

Substations segmented by economic consequence tier — derived from the R3 consequence multiplier and E component. Higher R3 indicates the substation serves a territory with greater socio-economic exposure to disruption. Israel's 4-tier R3 (1.06 / 1.05 / 1.04 / 1.03) is a moderate-upper gradient: GDP/capita spread between Tel Aviv (~$75-90k mehoz-level) and Northern/Southern (~$35-45k) is roughly 2×, comparable to the cohort mid-range.

Value of Lost Load (VoLL) context: Israel's industrial VoLL spans roughly 3-50 ₪/kWh (≈ €0.80-12/kWh) across mehozot — from Negev periphery agriculture and Eilat tourism at the low end (3-8 ₪/kWh) through Jerusalem government-services (8-18 ₪/kWh) and Haifa industrial + Central Sharon Plain tech corridors (15-30 ₪/kWh) to the Tel Aviv Silicon Wadi HQ cluster at the upper end (25-50 ₪/kWh; Check Point, Wix, Monday.com, Fiverr, Mobileye listings + TASE order flow). The high-tier band reflects R&D-intensive activity — Israel's R&D intensity ~5.4% of GDP is cohort-LEADING and consistently OECD-best.

B.3   Mehoz Cyber Posture Ranking

The 6 mehozot ranked by average R7 modifier — for Israel, lower values are rare given the nationwide INCD coverage and the Unit 8200 talent-pipeline footprint. The differentiator is sector concentration: Tel Aviv + Central host the Silicon Wadi cyber HQ cluster (Check Point + CyberArk + Wiz + Armis + dozens of mid-cap cyber-pure-plays), while Southern (Beersheba CyberSpark) hosts CERT-IL and the Ben-Gurion University cyber-engineering programme.
