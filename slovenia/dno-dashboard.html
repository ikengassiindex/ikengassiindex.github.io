Slovenia's distribution is split across five regional Elektro DSOs coordinated by SODO holding, with very different operating profiles. Elektro Ljubljana (33% share) covers the Osrednjeslovenska capital region plus parts of Gorenjska/Posavska/JV Slovenija; Elektro Maribor (25%) covers Podravska/Pomurska/Koroška including the Drava cascade; Elektro Celje (20%) is the Savinja Valley operator most exposed to the 2023 floods; Elektro Primorska (14%) operates the Adriatic coast and karst plateau; Elektro Gorenjska (8%) covers the Alpine valleys.

National SAIDI Context — CEE-South Cohort Comparison

SAIDI peer table

Slovenia's national SAIDI (~38 min/customer/year, AGEN-RS 2024) sits in the middle of the European range — better than Hungary (~57) and Poland (~95), worse than Germany (~12) and the Netherlands (~24). Urban Ljubljana achieves ~18 min (Elektro Ljubljana underground sections), while rural NE/SE territories average ~55 min (Elektro Maribor / Elektro Celje exposed to 2023-style flood events).

Krško NPP — Distribution-Side Impact on 5 DSOs

Single-unit cascade risk

Although Krško NPP is a transmission-level asset (operated by NEK / GEN Energija, dispatched by ELES), its single-unit nature affects DSO-level reliability via balancing reserve obligations. A Krško forced outage cascades to all 5 Elektro DSOs simultaneously. URSJV oversees nuclear safety; AGEN-RS handles the grid-side compensation arrangements.
