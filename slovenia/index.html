The SSI Index covers Slovenia's full transmission + distribution fleet across 12 NUTS-3 statistical regions, scoring each substation against six engineering and socio-economic components (C, V, I, E, S, T) and five modifiers (R3 consequence multiplier, R4 topology, R6 restoration, R6 seismic, R7 cyber). All inputs are drawn from 18 verified public sources — ELES (TSO), AGEN-RS (regulator), SURS (statistics), ARSO (weather + seismic), DRSV (floods), URSJV (Krško NPP oversight), SI-CERT (cyber) — making the methodology fully auditable.

Slovenia's defining hazard signature is the August 2023 Savinja floods (~€10 billion damages, the most severe natural disaster in modern Slovenian history) which anchors the R6c_flood modifier. Seismic exposure is calibrated against the 1976 Friuli M6.5 (IT) and 1998 Bovec M5.6 reference events, with the highest PGA in NW Soča valley + the Krško NPP zone (Posavska, single 696 MW PWR commissioned 1981, life-extended to 2043).

Latest Reference Event

August 2023 Savinja Basin Floods

~€10 billion in damages, ~6,000 buildings affected, 7 fatalities — the most severe natural disaster in modern Slovenian history. Epicentre: Savinjska + Osrednjeslovenska + Koroška. Defining anchor for R6c_flood modifier calibration. Grid impact: ~120 MV substations + 18 HV substations affected; full restoration took 6–10 weeks in worst-hit valleys. SSI weights this event as the dominant flood-modifier basis for Savinjska (α 0.10), Osrednjeslovenska + Podravska + Pomurska (α 0.08–0.09).
