Breaking the Silos

Power grid resilience is inseparable from its socio-economic context. A substation serving an energy-poor region with slow restoration times and weak fiscal capacity is fundamentally more vulnerable than one with identical electrical characteristics in a prosperous area. The SSI captures this reality by fusing traditional grid metrics with socio-economic, environmental, and energy-transition data — all from public sources.

Processing Pipeline
1

Ingest

101 variables from 28 verified public data sources — Enational system-research institute, REN, E-REDES + 12 DSOs, ADENE + Direção-Geral de Energia, INE Portugal, APA (APA INUNDA flood + ICNF + EFFIS PT wildfire), national forestry agency, national energy-research institute, ENTSO-E, Copernicus CDS / EFFIS / ERA5 Bourgouin, NASA FIRMS, EU-SILC + national household survey, OpenStreetMap, Eurostat, national system-research institute, national environment ministry, national geoportal, and others. Zero proprietary SCADA dependencies. Maximum ingestion frequency: weekly. v4.2 covers the 10,191-substation Portugal fleet end-to-end.

2

Normalise

Four normalisation methods: Method A (robust fleet percentile P5/P95), Method B (standard fleet percentile), Method C (bounded rescaling), and Method D (categorical mapping). Inverted metrics for density measures where higher = better resilience.

3

Weight

6-level hierarchical weighting across 6 components (C/V/I/E/S/T) and 20 metrics summing to R_base. Continuity dominates at 0.30, followed by Infrastructure (0.25) and Saturation (0.20). Weight budget validated by Sobol sensitivity analysis (verified 12 Jun 2026 against 10,191 Portugal substations; first-order S_i indices in Validation Framework below). The v4.2 Re composite aggregate is computed separately from the modifier family and surfaces as the 7th radar axis — it is NOT part of this weighting tree.

4

Compose R_base

Weighted sum of 6 normalised component scores produces the base resilience score.

5

Modify 11 modifiers

v4.0.2 baseline (five baseline factors): R3 (consequence + energy poverty), R4 (graph-theoretic network criticality), R6a (restoration speed, Enational system-research institute CAIDI), R6b (network topology — centrality + ring redundancy), R7 (digital readiness proxy).
v4.2 resilience family — six new resilience modifiers added to bring the total to 11 modifiers: R6c_flood (APA INUNDA additive cliff +0.00..+0.30, P3=high/P2=medium/P1=low per Portugal convention) · R6d_wildfire (Canadian Fire Weather Index via EFFIS + NASA FIRMS + ICNF + EFFIS PT national-tier, multiplicative [1.00, 1.20]) · R6e_winter (Bourgouin freezing-rain p99 tail-event amplifier per Bourgouin 2000 + Birk-Lenning 2021, multiplicative [1.00, 1.15]; Portugal collapses to ~1.005 per brief §6 methodologically-defensible absence) · R8_adapt (adaptive capacity from Enational system-research institute bulk smart meter rollout + national energy-research institute + DESI) · R9_compound (pairwise hazard coupling — R6b×R6c, R6c×R6d, R6c×R6e) · R10_just (energy justice from EU-SILC + national energy-poverty research).

5b

Re composite v4.2 — bounded resilience aggregate

The Re composite is the bounded resilience aggregate that captures the v4.2 modifier family in a single scalar:
Re_raw = (R6d × R6e × R8 × R9 × R10) + (R6c − 1.00) Bounds: [0.920, 1.787] (Re_raw can fall below 1.000 when R8_adapt < 1.00 dominates other near-neutral factors). Normalised: Re_norm = clip((Re_raw − 0.920) / (1.787 − 0.920), 0, 1). Portugal fleet observed range (post-Path-B, canonical-computed from ssi-data.json.v4.2): low-exposure reference station Re_raw 1.097 / Re_norm 0.204; high-exposure reference station Re_raw 1.477 / Re_norm 0.642.

6

Monte Carlo

10,000 iterations per substation using a 20×20 Gaussian copula correlation matrix. Captures measurement, spatial, and staleness uncertainty. Produces median, P5, P95, skewness, and P_critical for each substation.

7

Classify

Four bands: Low (0.00–0.25), Medium (0.25–0.50), High (0.50–0.75), Critical (0.75–1.00). Alert flags trigger when any single component exceeds its P95 fleet threshold.

Why the v4.2 form distinguishes multiplicative from additive. R6c (flood) is the only additive modifier — high-flood substations would otherwise be silently clipped by soft_clip_upper, hiding genuine hazard. Placing the R6c shift OUTSIDE the clip preserves the full +0.00…+0.30 range in the final score. The other ten modifiers remain inside the clip because their bounded multiplicative behaviour is well-defined for the soft-clip compression. All bounds, vintages, and data sources are documented in the Modifiers table below.

Uncertainty Quantification

Monte Carlo engine

Every substation score includes a 90% confidence interval derived from 10,000 Monte Carlo iterations. The simulation uses a 20×20 Gaussian copula to preserve empirical correlations between metrics (e.g., ρ(C1, E1) ≈ 0.70–0.85). Three uncertainty sources are modelled: measurement error, spatial disaggregation noise, and data staleness.

Competitive Positioning

v4.2

SSI v4.2 extends the v4.0.2 baseline (which scored competitively or leads on 15 of 16 dimensions versus CEER, CIGRE/IEEE, EPRI, DOE/PNNL GMLC, JRC DSO Observatory, and academic MCDA frameworks) with the resilience-modifier family (R6c flood / R6d wildfire / R6e winter / R8 adapt / R9 compound / R10 just), the Re composite (bounded resilience aggregate of the v4.2 modifier family; surfaced as the 7th radar axis but methodologically a modifier-family aggregate, NOT a 7th SSI component), and the W1–W10 anti-maladaptation governance gate (5/1/1/3 4-tier mesh resolution per Convention #6 §5.6).
