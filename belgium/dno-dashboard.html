DSO Segmented Dashboard — Belgium

11 DSOs · 5.85 M LV customers · 11 Provinces · Federated under Synergrid

Belgium's distribution market is federated: three regional DSOs (Fluvius in Flanders, ORES in Wallonia, Sibelga in Brussels-Capital) cover 99% of LV customers, with eight smaller Walloon inter-municipals (RESA, AIEG, AIESH, REW, IDEFIN, IDEG, AIE, Régie de Wavre) serving the remaining 1%. The transmission backbone is operated by Elia (380 / 150 / 70 kV). The page below shows the three dominant DSOs side-by-side, then drill-down comparisons.

Why the SAIDI spread? — Network archetype

The 2.2× SAIDI gap between Sibelga and ORES is dominated by network archetype and underground share, not management quality:

  • Sibelga — urban underground. 92% of MV cabling underground in Brussels-Capital, fault rates ~1/10 of overhead. The 19-commune region is a single contiguous urban service area with concentrated maintenance and short response times. 700k customers on 162 km² gives the highest customer density in BE.
  • Fluvius — Flemish mixed. 62% underground, predominantly underground in city cores (Antwerp, Ghent, Bruges) but overhead in rural Limburg + Kempen. Flanders' flat terrain and dense population keeps restoration times short.
  • ORES — Walloon overhead. 31% underground, dominated by overhead lines in Ardennes (Luxembourg-BE) and mid-density rural Wallonia (Hainaut, Namur). The province of Luxembourg-BE faces the highest restoration times during winter storms — its low population density spreads customers over large distances.

The 8 Walloon inter-municipals (RESA / AIEG / AIESH / REW / IDEFIN / IDEG / AIE / Régie de Wavre) cover ~185k customers (~3% of WA) with slightly worse SAIDI (~29 min), folded into the ORES regional average per d02b_synergrid weighting.

Per Italy Formula Construct §4 R6, the operating-stress modifier penalises overhead-heavy archetypes proportionally — see the modifier R6 column in Intelligence.

SAIDI in European context

Belgium's national SAIDI (22 min, weighted by customer share) places it 3rd in the EU after Germany (12 min) and Netherlands (18.5 min). Continued post-2000 underground migration (mostly Flanders + Brussels) and ENTSO-E Continental Europe synchronous backing keep the grid resilient despite the upcoming 2025–2035 nuclear phase-out (Doel 1/2/4 + Tihange 1/3 — 40% of generation today).

Nuclear phase-out outlook — operational risk to grid resilience

Belgium's nuclear fleet supplies ~40% of generation (Doel 1/2/4 + Tihange 1/3 — Doel 3 and Tihange 2 already shut down). The 2022 federal agreement extends Doel 4 + Tihange 3 to 2035; the others phase out by 2025–2027. Three implications for grid resilience (R6 + R7 modifiers):

  • Replacement-capacity build-out — 2 GW offshore wind (Princess Elisabeth Zone, COD 2027), new gas-fired flexibility, plus increased BE-FR/NL/DE imports via Pentalateral CWE flow-based coupling. Each MW of nuclear baseload retired requires ~1.4 MW of intermittent + flexible replacement.
  • Transmission stress at NPP grid nodes — Doel 380 kV switchyard near Antwerp port and Tihange 380 kV near Liège will see lower utilisation, but legacy reactor-side equipment continues to age in place during phase-out — captured in Markov ETTC.
  • Cross-border exposure — Cattenom NPP (EDF, France) sits ~50 km from BE southern border with 4 reactors operating until ~2050; while not in BE's R6b PGA framework, it factors into the radiological-context narrative for southern provinces (Luxembourg-BE, Namur).
