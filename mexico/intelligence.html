The SSI Index is the only operational framework that fuses substation-level engineering metrics with socio-economic consequence valuation in a single composite score — scoring 71/80 (89%) across 16 competitive dimensions. Its engine processes ~52,000 source data points per run, executes ~31 million Monte Carlo simulations through a 20×20 Gaussian copula correlation matrix, and performs ~400 million arithmetic operations — producing ~232,000 output data points with full uncertainty quantification across 3,140 substations and ~8,500 grid lines spanning ~30,000 km.

Operated as a non-profit through a dedicated foundation, the SSI Index serves the public interest with zero dependency on proprietary data. All 95 variables are drawn from 30+ verified public sources (CENACE, CRE, CFE, INEGI, SMN, CENAPRED), making the methodology fully auditable and reproducible. Initially covering Mexico's CENACE-managed transmission and ~250 distribution network operators (DSOs), the Index is progressively being rolled out to all OECD countries — applying the same silo-breaking approach: fusing grid risk with societal exposure at asset-level resolution wherever open data permits.

Section B — Recurring Module

Cyber & Economic Exposure Monitor

Why this section exists: Traditional grid risk frameworks treat cybersecurity and economic vulnerability as separate domains. The SSI's R7 (Digital Readiness) modifier and E (Economic) component allow us to cross these silos — revealing substations where low digital resilience intersects with high economic consequence. This is precisely the anticipatory intelligence that Mexico's Energy Regulatory Commission (CRE) and network operator strategic planning requires.

Data note: This inaugural edition uses Edition 001 baseline data. Subsequent editions will track month-over-month changes in cyber exposure and economic consequence measures. Subscribe to updates to follow this intelligence as the monitoring matures.

Section D

Deep-Dive: Gulf Coast–Pacific Corridor Risk Assessment

The Gulf Coast–Pacific Corridor — spanning Veracruz, Guerrero, Oaxaca, and surrounding Estados — faces compounded natural hazards that make grid resilience particularly challenging. This corridor experiences both Atlantic hurricane season (August–October) and Pacific hurricane season (May–November), with seismic activity from three major fault zones, plus geothermal stress in volcanic regions. The SSI R6b modifier is weighted especially heavily in this geography to capture the interaction of these hazards with substation topology and restoration capacity.
