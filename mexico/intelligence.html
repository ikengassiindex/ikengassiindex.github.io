The SSI Index is the only operational framework that fuses substation-level engineering metrics with socio-economic consequence valuation in a single composite score — scoring 71/80 (89%) across 16 competitive dimensions. Its engine processes ~52,000 source data points per run, executes ~31 million Monte Carlo simulations through a 20×20 Gaussian copula correlation matrix, and performs ~400 million arithmetic operations — producing ~232,000 output data points with full uncertainty quantification across 3,140 substations and ~30,396 grid lines spanning ~30,000 km.

Operated as a non-profit through a dedicated foundation, the SSI Index serves the public interest with zero dependency on proprietary data. All 95 variables are drawn from 30+ verified public sources (CENACE, CRE, CFE, INEGI, SMN, CENAPRED), making the methodology fully auditable and reproducible. Initially covering Mexico's CENACE-managed transmission and ~250 CFE distribution divisions (DSOs), the Index is progressively being rolled out to all OECD countries — applying the same silo-breaking approach: fusing grid risk with societal exposure at asset-level resolution wherever open data permits.

Section D

Deep-Dive: The Gulf Coast–Pacific Corridor

The Gulf Coast–Pacific Corridor — spanning Veracruz, Guerrero, Oaxaca, Tabasco, and surrounding estados — faces a uniquely compounded hazard environment that makes grid resilience particularly challenging. This geographic corridor experiences both Atlantic hurricane season (August–October) and simultaneous Pacific hurricane season (May–November), creating a six-month window of dual storm exposure. Overlaid on this are three major seismic fault systems (Mexico-Cocos boundary, Venta Rica fault, and Tehuantepec Ridge), plus ongoing geothermal stress in volcanic regions (Trans-Mexican Belt). The convergence of these natural hazards — each independently capable of causing cascading failures — makes the Corridor a critical case study in the SSI framework.

The R6b modifier is weighted especially heavily in this geography, using SMN hurricane historical reanalysis and CENAPRED seismic scenario models to capture the interaction of compound hazards with substation topology and restoration capacity. CENACE's post-2019 hardening investments have focused on this corridor, recognizing that a simultaneous multi-state outage (triggered by a major hurricane-seismic coupled event) would ripple across the entire Baja California–Texas cross-border power exchange and disrupt North American manufacturing supply chains dependent on Mexican energy stability.

Corridor-level analysis reveals substations where high exposure to compound natural hazards intersects with limited restoration pathways. The SSI's asset-level resolution identifies critical nodes where grid hardening investment yields maximum resilience return — a strategic advantage over traditional regional blackout risk models which operate at lower resolution.

Section E

Americas Context Card

How does Mexico's grid resilience compare to its peers in the Americas? Benchmarking System Average Interruption Duration Index (SAIDI) across regional grids reveals Mexico's operational challenge: the national fleet median SAIDI of ~240 minutes sits between the better-served US urban centers (85 min) and larger Latin American economies like Brazil (~400 min). However, aggregated metrics mask acute geographic disparity — Mexico City metropolitan SAIDI (~85 min, approaching OECD urban norms) contrasts sharply with rural Mexico (~600 min), signaling that the SSI's regional disaggregation is essential for targeting investment.

SSI Enhanced Neural Network — Monthly Topic

The SSI combines six risk components (C, I, S, V, E, T) into a single composite R score, then applies five geographic and operational modifiers (R3–R7) to capture context. The result is a probabilistic risk profile — not a point estimate, but a distribution with quantified uncertainty. This section explores the interaction between components and modifiers through the lens of data quality and confidence.

DCI — Data Confidence Index

Measures the completeness and freshness of input data for each substation. Ranges from 0 (no data) to 1.0 (full data across all 95 variables). Mexico\'s fleet exhibits an average DCI of 0.68, reflecting mature CENACE transmission data but gaps in some remote CFE distribution areas.

Looking Ahead

Q2 2026 Roadmap: Mexico Edition Maturation

  • CENACE SCADA modernization integration (May 2026): Real-time dispatch data feed from CENACE's next-generation operational technology (OT) systems will expand R7 cyber readiness assessment beyond broadband/smart meter proxies to include live OT security baselines and incident logs.
  • Hurricane season preparedness (June–November 2026): Monthly updates to R6b modifier incorporating live SMN forecast models and historical reanalysis; seasonal risk escalation alerts for Gulf Coast and Pacific corridor substations as hurricane probability enters peak window.
  • SENER energy reform alignment (Q2 2026): Integrate newly published SENER energy security strategy and investment priorities into SSI framework, ensuring the Index reflects Mexico's policy-driven energy transition goals alongside technical risk assessment.
  • CRE compliance framework integration (Q2 2026): Align SSI data source registry and reporting structure with CRE's expanded 2026 digital readiness and reliability standards, enabling regulators to cross-reference SSI asset scores with CRE filings.
  • Mexico–US interconnection modeling (Q3 2026): Launch cross-border substation analysis covering WECC-linked Baja California transmission corridors, Mexican generator interconnections with US markets, and cascading failure scenarios triggering cross-border impacts.

Long-term Vision (2027–2028)

The SSI Mexico edition is designed as a living system, not a static report. Over the next two years, we anticipate integrating machine learning workflows that track component trends across monthly editions, flag anomalies (e.g., sudden E component inflation in a region), and predict high-risk transitions before they materialize operationally. CENACE and CFE distribution operators will leverage the SSI's asset-level resolution to inform their own internal capital prioritization, creating a feedback loop where the Index becomes not just an external research tool but an embedded instrument in Mexico's grid governance.

We also anticipate the Index will catalyze community-level engagement: municipalities and estado governments increasingly recognize that grid resilience is a public good tied to economic development, disaster preparedness, and energy equity. The SSI's transparent methodology and reproducible data sources make it amenable to stakeholder audits — a critical feature in building trust and policy adoption.
