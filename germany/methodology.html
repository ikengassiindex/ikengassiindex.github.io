Methodology

How the SSI v4.0.2 is calculated for Germany — from raw data to final classification. Open methodology, open data, fully reproducible. 5-tier R3 calibration reflects DE's East/West socio-economic differential (KB §54).

Breaking the Silos

Power grid resilience is inseparable from its socio-economic context. A Landkreis serving an energy-poor region with slow restoration times and weak fiscal capacity is fundamentally more vulnerable than one with identical electrical characteristics in a prosperous area. The SSI captures this reality by fusing traditional grid metrics with socio-economic, environmental, and energy-transition data — all from public sources. For Germany specifically, the 5-tier R3 calibration captures the post-reunification East/West differential that 4-tier models miss.

Processing Pipeline
1

Ingest

95 variables from 35 verified public data sources — BNetzA, DESTATIS, the 4 TSO Netzentwicklungsplane (50Hertz · Amprion · TenneT TSO DE · TransnetBW), DWD, BGR, BfG + LfU Bayern, dena, BSI KRITIS, BMU, Bundesbank, OSM Overpass API, Copernicus CDS, and others. Zero proprietary SCADA dependencies. Maximum ingestion frequency: weekly.

2

Normalise

Four normalisation methods: Method A (robust fleet percentile P5/P95), Method B (standard fleet percentile), Method C (bounded rescaling), and Method D (categorical mapping). Inverted metrics for density measures where higher = better resilience.

3

Weight

6-level hierarchical weighting across 6 components and 20 metrics. Continuity dominates at 0.30, followed by Infrastructure (0.25) and Saturation (0.20). Weight budget validated by Sobol sensitivity analysis.

4

Compose R_base

Weighted sum of 6 normalised component scores produces the base resilience score.

5

Modify

Seven multiplicative modifiers adjust R_base for the German context: R2 (adaptive climate IRI), R3 (consequence + energy poverty · 5-tier 1.02 / 1.04 / 1.06 / 1.08 / 1.10 per §54 calibration — DE East/West differential), R4 (graph-theoretic network criticality), R6a (restoration speed · BNetzA Monitoring), R6b (Rhine Graben seismic, α∈[0.02, 0.12]), R6c (flood — Rhine + Elbe + Danube + North Sea storm surge; largest exposure footprint in DACH), R7 (digital readiness · ceiling 1.06, DESI 0.61, NIS2 transposed Q4 2024 late EU; 4 TSO + 800 DSO attack surface). Plus 5 micro-enrichments for vulnerable populations, flood zones, landslides, digital capabilities, and healthcare criticality.

6

Monte Carlo

10,000 iterations per Landkreis using a 20×20 Gaussian copula correlation matrix. Captures measurement, spatial, and staleness uncertainty. Produces median, P5, P95, skewness, and P_critical for each Landkreis.

7

Classify

Four bands: Low (0.00–0.25), Medium (0.25–0.50), High (0.50–0.75), Critical (0.75–1.00). Alert flags trigger when any single component exceeds its P95 fleet threshold.

Uncertainty Quantification

Monte Carlo engine

Every Landkreis score includes a 90% confidence interval derived from 10,000 Monte Carlo iterations. The simulation uses a 20×20 Gaussian copula to preserve empirical correlations between metrics (e.g., ρ(C1, E1) ≈ 0.70–0.85). Three uncertainty sources are modelled: measurement error, spatial disaggregation noise, and data staleness.

Why 5 Tiers? The Germany East/West Differential

§54 calibration narrative

Germany is the only DACH country whose R3 consequence calibration requires a fifth tier. Lithuania, Latvia, Austria, and (in the planned CH refresh) Switzerland all fit the 4-tier 1.02 / 1.04 / 1.06 / 1.08 ladder. Germany's post-reunification socio-economic geography demands an additional 1.10 Light-Rural former-East tier covering Brandenburg, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, Sachsen-Anhalt, Thüringen, and the rural parts of Sachsen. Three structural facts drive this departure:

  • Persistent income gap. Per-capita GDP in the eastern Länder remains ~25% below the western average 35 years after reunification. WIFO + Bundesbank regional accounts confirm the gap is structural, not cyclical.
  • Coal phase-out concentration. The Lausitz Just Transition Fund (€40B+, 2038 coal phase-out target) is geographically concentrated in Brandenburg + Sachsen. Substations serving these regions face simultaneous economic transition stress (industrial decline) and grid topology stress (lignite-mine substation closures).
  • Energy poverty headroom. DESTATIS Mikrozensus consistently records higher energy-poverty incidence in former-East Länder (12–15%) than former-West (6–9%). The 1.10 tier captures this Landkreis-vulnerability headroom that would otherwise be capped at 1.08.

Practically, the 5-tier ladder shifts ~8–12% of eastern-Länder substations from "Light-Industrial 1.08" into the higher "Light-Rural 1.10" bracket, which materially affects the R_final ordering when CI overlap is narrow. Visual representation in intelligence.html B.2 Business Fabric shows 5 bars (not 4) to reflect this calibration.
