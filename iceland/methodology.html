Methodology

How the SSI v4.0.2 is calculated — from raw data to final classification. Open methodology, open data, fully reproducible.

Breaking the Silos

Power grid resilience is inseparable from its socio-economic context. A NUTS-3 landshluti serving an energy-poor region with slow restoration times and weak fiscal capacity is fundamentally more vulnerable than one with identical electrical characteristics in a prosperous area. The SSI captures this reality by fusing traditional grid metrics with socio-economic, environmental, and energy-transition data — all from public sources.

Processing Pipeline
1

Ingest

95 variables from 30+ verified public data sources — Landsnet network statement, Orkustofnun raforkutölfræði + quality reports, Veitur + RARIK + HS Veitur DSO quarterly filings, Hagstofa Íslands landshluti regional accounts, Seðlabanki Íslands national accounts, Veðurstofa Íslands (IMO/OVF) consolidated meteorology + hydrology + seismic + volcanic monitoring (Þjórsá Q100 + Skeiðará/Markarfljót jökulhlaup + Mid-Atlantic Ridge SISZ/TFZ catalogue + Reykjanes/Vatnajökull/Hekla networks), Umhverfisstofnun (UST) air quality + ISO 9223 corrosion, CERT-IS + Fjarskiptastofa (ECOI) cyber posture, Copernicus, Eurostat, IEA/OECD, OSM Overpass. Iceland is NOT an ENTSO-E member (insular grid). Zero proprietary SCADA dependencies. Maximum ingestion frequency: daily.

2

Normalise

Four normalisation methods: Method A (robust fleet percentile P5/P95), Method B (standard fleet percentile), Method C (bounded rescaling), and Method D (categorical mapping). Inverted metrics for density measures where higher = better resilience.

3

Weight

6-level hierarchical weighting across 6 components and 20 metrics. Continuity dominates at 0.30, followed by Infrastructure (0.25) and Saturation (0.20). Weight budget validated by Sobol sensitivity analysis.

4

Compose R_base

Weighted sum of 6 normalised component scores produces the base resilience score.

5

Modify

Seven multiplicative modifiers adjust R_base for context: R2 (adaptive climate IRI — at 64-66°N heat-wave loading is structurally low, winter windstorm + atmospheric icing dominate), R3 (consequence + energy poverty — Iceland uses a 4-tier calibration reflecting the flat Reykjavík-vs-rural GDP gradient and 3-smelter industrial concentration), R4 (graph-theoretic network criticality on an insular grid with zero cross-border interconnects), R6_seismic (Mid-Atlantic Ridge SISZ + TFZ — highest seismic α in cohort outside NZ), R6_volcanic (NEW sub-pattern — Reykjanes/Vatnajökull/Hekla active volcanism), R6c_jokulhlaup (NEW sub-pattern — glacial-outburst floods replace conventional Q100 fluvial), R7 (digital readiness, ceiling 1.04).

6

Monte Carlo

10,000 iterations per landshluti using a 20×20 Gaussian copula correlation matrix. Captures measurement, spatial, and staleness uncertainty. Produces median, P5, P95, skewness, and P_critical for each NUTS-3 landshluti.

7

Classify

Four bands: Low (0.00–0.25), Medium (0.25–0.50), High (0.50–0.75), Critical (0.75–1.00). Alert flags trigger when any single component exceeds its P95 fleet threshold.

ModifierDomainRangeWhat It Captures
R2 Adaptive IRI + Climate internal Shifts weight from climate-IRI to structural metrics where local climate risk is low. CMIP6 SSP2-4.5 projections adjust forward-looking risk.
R3 Consequence + Poverty [0.70, 1.30] Amplifies risk for landshluti serving large/energy-poor populations with high economic dependency. Iceland uses a 4-tier calibration (Industrial-Aluminum 1.05 / Capital-Reykjavík 1.04 / Commercial-SME 1.03 / Light-Rural 1.02) — the flattest gradient in cohort. Industrial-Aluminum tier reflects the 3-smelter corridors (Alcoa Fjarðaál, Rio Tinto ISAL Straumsvík, Century Aluminum Norðurál Grundartangi) consuming ~70-80% of national electricity; Capital-Reykjavík reflects ~64% of population in Höfuðborgarsvæðið.
R4 Graph Criticality [0.80, 1.35] Penalises topological bottlenecks: high betweenness centrality, bridge nodes, low degree. Built from OSM power graph (687 nodes · 4,261 edges).
R6a Restoration Speed [0.90, 1.10] Orkustofnun-CAIDI-based: rewards fast-restoring urban areas (Reykjavík metro under Veitur), penalises slow ones. Vestfirðir (Westfjords, <7,000 population in 22,000 km²) and Norðurland vestra carry a remote-rural access penalty via long radial 132 kV feeds.
R6_seismic Network Topology + Seismic [1.00, 1.25] Network centrality + Mid-Atlantic Ridge seismic hazard. Iceland straddles the ridge with two active source zones — the South Iceland Seismic Zone (SISZ, anchor: 29 May 2008 Mw 6.3 doublet Selfoss-Hveragerði) and the Tjörnes Fracture Zone (TFZ, Norðurland eystra). R6_seismic α band [0.05, 0.20] — HIGHEST in cohort outside New Zealand. Veðurstofa Íslands publishes the 475-yr PGA hazard maps.
R6_volcanic Volcanic Activity (NEW) [1.00, 1.20] FIRST IN COHORT — codifies active-volcanism exposure on grid infrastructure. R6_volcanic α band [0.00, 0.20]: Reykjanes/Sundhnúkur peak 0.14 (active eruption cycle since 19 Mar 2021; 8 Feb 2024 eruption damaged Svartsengi hot-water pipeline, ~26,000 residents lost district heating ~4 days, Grindavík evacuated since 10 Nov 2023); Suðurland 0.10 (Hekla / Eyjafjallajökull 2010 / Katla overdue); Austurland 0.07 (Vatnajökull / Grímsvötn / Bárðarbunga Holuhraun 2014-2015); Vestfirðir + Norðurland vestra 0.00-0.02 (no active volcanism).
R6c_jokulhlaup Glacial-Outburst Flood (NEW) [1.00, 1.15] FIRST IN COHORT — Iceland's flood risk is structurally jökulhlaup-dominant, not Q100-fluvial. Sub-glacial volcanic activity melts ice within Vatnajökull / Mýrdalsjökull / Eyjafjallajökull, water accumulates in a sub-glacial lake, then bursts as a flash flood. Anchors: 1996 Skeiðará jökulhlaup (Grímsvötn-triggered, destroyed Ring Road bridges); 14 April 2010 Eyjafjallajökull (Markarfljót valley flooded). α band [0.00, 0.15] concentrated in Suðurland + Austurland 132 kV transmission corridors.
R7 Digital Readiness [0.99, 1.04] Regional DESI digital readiness score (Iceland EFTA-EEA proxy ~0.65-0.70), CERT-IS maturity (founded 2013, FIRST + Nordic CERT Cooperation member), NIS2 transposition (Act 78/2019 + 2025 amendment via EEA process, in force 1 Jan 2026; Fjarskiptastofa/ECOI is the competent authority). Ceiling 1.04 matches SI; above SK 1.03 — reflects Iceland's OECD top-5 fiber penetration >99% and digital-government ~95% adoption despite later CERT founding date.

Uncertainty Quantification

Monte Carlo engine

Every landshluti score includes a 90% confidence interval derived from 10,000 Monte Carlo iterations. The simulation uses a 20×20 Gaussian copula to preserve empirical correlations between metrics (e.g., ρ(C1, E1) ≈ 0.70–0.85). Three uncertainty sources are modelled: measurement error, spatial disaggregation noise, and data staleness.
