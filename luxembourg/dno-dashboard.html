Operator Profile — Luxembourg

Single combined TSO+DSO · 308,000 customers · 12 cantons

Unlike multi-DSO markets such as France (Enedis-dominated with local régies), Czechia (ČEZ Distribuce / EG.D / PREdistribuce), or Germany (≈900 DSOs), Luxembourg is served by a single combined operator: Creos Luxembourg S.A. It is simultaneously the transmission system operator (TSO, 220/65 kV backbone connected to the DE-LU joint bidding zone) and the distribution system operator (DSO, 20 kV and LV) for the entire country. The page below summarises Creos at the national level, then surfaces the side-by-side comparison against neighbouring single-op and multi-op markets.

Why such a low SAIDI? — Network archetype

Luxembourg's 24.7 min/yr SAIDI ranks behind Germany (~12) but ahead of all of its other neighbours. Three structural reasons:

  • Compact, mostly-underground urban core. Luxembourg City + Esch-sur-Alzette + Differdange + Dudelange concentrate the bulk of customers in dense networks with high underground share — fault rates are an order of magnitude below overhead.
  • Single operator, integrated planning. Creos is simultaneously TSO and DSO; the 220 kV / 65 kV / 20 kV layers are planned together, reducing interface losses. There is no "DSO–TSO border" zone where coordination friction typically lengthens outages in larger countries.
  • Young fleet. Most of the LU grid was rebuilt or reinforced after 2000 (post the 1999 storm Lothar) and again after the 2018 DE-LU bidding-zone integration. Markov ETTC therefore comes in around 12 yr, higher than the CZ baseline ~9 yr.

The remaining 24.7 min is dominated by overhead MV in the Ardennes-Oesling north (Clervaux, Wiltz, Vianden) where line lengths-per-customer are higher and winter storm restoration is slower. See the modifier R6 column in Intelligence.

SAIDI in European context

Luxembourg's national SAIDI (24.7 min) places it in the second tier among Continental European systems — behind Germany and the Netherlands, ahead of Belgium / Austria / France / Czechia. The compact territory and post-2000 rebuild are the dominant drivers; the operator structure (single combined TSO+DSO) is a contributing factor but is not unique to top performers.
